Stair Layout: The "Rule of 18"

Stair building is precise work. If one step is 1/4 inch different from the others, it creates a trip hazard. Building codes are very strict about this "consistency."

How to Measure Total Rise

The biggest mistake is measuring from the subfloor. You must measure from Finished Floor to Finished Floor .

Example: If you are planning to add hardwood on the top floor and tile on the bottom floor later, you must add that thickness (e.g., 3/4") to your measurements now . If you don't, the top and bottom steps will be the wrong height when the flooring is installed.

Cutting the Stringer

The stringer is the diagonal 2x12 that holds the weight.

  • Drop the Stringer: When cutting the very bottom of the stringer, you must slice off the thickness of one tread (e.g., 1 inch). If you forget this, your bottom step will be 1 inch too tall.
  • Open vs. Closed: An "Open Stringer" has the sawtooth notches cut out. A "Closed Stringer" (housed) has the treads routed into the side of the board.

Headroom Clearance

Building codes require a minimum of 6 feet 8 inches (80") of headroom measured vertically from the nose of the tread to the ceiling above. Always check this before you commit to a steep stair layout.
