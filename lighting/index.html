Designing the Perfect Recessed Lighting Grid

Recessed lighting (often called "can lights" or "pot lights") is the gold standard for modern home illumination. It makes ceilings look higher and rooms feel larger. However, a poor layout creates the dreaded "Swiss Cheese Effect" (too many holes) or "Cave Effect" (shadowy corners). Use this guide to plan a grid that provides even, shadow-free light.

1 The "Half-Height" Rule

The most common question is: "How far apart should they be?" The industry standard rule of thumb is simple:

Spacing Distance = Ceiling Height ÷ 2

Example: If you have 8-foot ceilings, space your lights 4 feet apart. If you have 10-foot ceilings, space them 5 feet apart. This ensures the "cones" of light overlap slightly at floor level, eliminating dark spots.

2 Avoid the Wall Scallop

A critical mistake is placing lights too close to the wall. This creates a "scalloping" effect—harsh U-shaped shadows near the ceiling that highlight every imperfection in your paint or drywall tape.

The Sweet Spot

Ideally, the distance from the wall to the first row of lights should be half the distance between lights.

(e.g., If lights are 4ft apart, place them 2ft from the wall).

Kitchen Cabinets

In kitchens, measure from the edge of the counter , not the wall. You want the light to shine on your workspace, not behind your head (which creates shadows while chopping).

3 4-inch vs. 6-inch Cans

Traditionally, 6-inch cans were standard. Today, the trend has shifted.

  • 6-Inch Lights Best for general lighting in large rooms (Living Rooms, Vaulted Ceilings). Wide beam spread covers more area.
  • 4-Inch Lights Best for task lighting (over Kitchen Counters) or small rooms. They look more modern and discreet but you need more of them to light the same space.
  • Wafer (Canless) LEDs The new DIY favorite. These are ultra-thin (1/2 inch) and clip directly onto the drywall. They fit under joists, meaning you never have to worry about hitting a stud during layout.

Choosing Color Temperature (Kelvin)

The "color" of the light changes the mood entirely. Most modern LEDs have a switch on the back to select the temp.

2700K - 3000K Warm White

Bedrooms & Living Rooms. Cozy and relaxing.

3500K - 4000K Cool White

Kitchens & Bathrooms. Clean and energetic.

5000K+ Daylight

Garages & Basements only. Can feel "clinical" inside.

5. The Golden Rule: Always Install Dimmers

Warning: LED lights behave differently than old bulbs. Do not use an old rotary dimmer switch. You must buy a dimmer rated for "CFL/LED" .

If you use a non-compatible dimmer, your new lights will flicker, buzz, or fail to turn off completely. Lutron Diva or Caseta are the industry standards for LED compatibility.
