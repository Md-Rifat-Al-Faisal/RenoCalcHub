How to Build a Retaining Wall That Won't Fall

[Image of retaining wall cross section]

A retaining wall fights gravity and water pressure 24/7. If you just stack stones on the dirt, the wall will bulge and collapse after the first heavy rain. You must follow the "3 Rules of Stability."

Rule 1: The Buried Course

The bottom row of blocks should never be seen. It must be buried to lock the wall into the earth.

Rule of Thumb: Bury 1 inch of wall for every 8 inches of height.
Example: A 24-inch tall wall needs at least 3 inches buried (so 1 full block course).

Rule 2: Drainage is Everything

Water is heavy. If water gets trapped behind your wall, it exerts hydrostatic pressure that pushes the wall over.

  • Drainage Stone: You must backfill the space directly behind the blocks with 12 inches of clean gravel (usually #57 stone). Do not use dirt! Dirt traps water; gravel lets it drain.
  • Drain Pipe: For walls taller than 3 feet, install a perforated drain pipe at the bottom of the gravel layer to carry water away.

Rule 3: Level Base

Start with a 6-inch deep trench filled with "Paver Base" (compactable gravel). Use a 4-foot level to ensure your base course is perfectly dead level. If the bottom row is crooked, the top row will be a disaster.

Do You Need Geogrid?

Geogrid is a plastic mesh that anchors the wall into the soil behind it.
Under 3 Feet: Usually not needed (gravity holds it).
Over 3 Feet: Mandatory. Check local codes.
