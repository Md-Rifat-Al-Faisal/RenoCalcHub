The Ultimate Framing Guide: 16" vs 24" Spacing

[Image of wall framing diagram with labels]

Framing a wall looks simple—just vertical sticks—but the structural integrity of your house depends on getting the spacing and headers right.

What is "On Center" (OC)?

"16 inches On Center" means the center of one stud is exactly 16 inches away from the center of the next stud.

16" OC (Standard)

Required for load-bearing walls and exterior walls. It provides more strength and nailing surface for siding/drywall.

24" OC (Advanced)

Often allowed for interior partition walls (non-load bearing). It saves money on lumber and leaves more room for insulation.

The "Three Plate" System

A proper wall is not just a rectangle. It has three horizontal plates:

  • Bottom Plate (Sole Plate): Treated lumber if touching concrete. Anchors the wall to the floor.
  • Top Plate: Nailing surface for the ceiling drywall.
  • Double Top Plate: A second layer on top. This overlaps corners and intersections to tie separate walls together structurally.

Why Buy 92-5/8" Studs?

You might think you should buy 8-foot (96") studs for an 8-foot ceiling. This is wrong.

An 8-foot wall includes the 3 plates (which are 1.5" thick each).
3 Plates x 1.5" = 4.5" total plate height.
96" (Total Wall) - 4.5" (Plates) = 91.5" Stud Space .

Lumber yards sell "Pre-Cut Studs" at exactly 92-5/8" to perfectly fit standard 8ft walls with a little wiggle room for flooring. Buying these saves you from cutting every single board.

Corners and Tees

You cannot just put one stud in a corner. You need a "California Corner" (3 studs nailed in a U-shape).

Why? Because once the drywall is up on one wall, you need a wooden lip sticking out to nail the drywall on the other wall. Our calculator automatically adds 2-3 extra studs for every corner to account for this.
