Lawn Guide: Seed vs. Sod

A lush green lawn is the American dream, but getting there is tricky. You have two choices: Seed (Cheap & Slow) or Sod (Expensive & Instant) .

Seeding: New vs. Overseeding

New Lawn

Rate: 8-10 lbs per 1,000 sq ft.
Used on bare dirt. Requires hay/straw covering to keep moisture in. Takes 4-6 weeks to establish.

Overseeding

Rate: 4-5 lbs per 1,000 sq ft.
Used on existing thin grass. You must aerate (poke holes) first so the seeds touch the soil.

Which Grass Species?

The type of seed determines the weight you buy. Some seeds are tiny (Bluegrass), while others are large (Fescue).

  • Tall Fescue: Tough, drought-resistant. Best for high traffic. Large seeds = heavy application rate.
  • Kentucky Bluegrass: Beautiful, dark green. Spreads on its own. Tiny seeds = lighter application rate.
  • Bermuda/Zoysia: Warm-season grasses (South). Go brown in winter. Very dense.

Sod Installation Tips

🧱 Brick Pattern

Never line up the seams of your sod. Lay the rolls in a "brick pattern" (staggered).

Why? If you line up the seams, water will create a channel and wash the dirt away, leaving gaps. Staggering locks the lawn together.

Watering Rule of Thumb

Seed: Water 2x daily (lightly) to keep the surface damp. If the seed dries out once, it dies.
Sod: Water deeply 1x daily. You need to soak through the sod into the dirt below to encourage roots to dig down.
