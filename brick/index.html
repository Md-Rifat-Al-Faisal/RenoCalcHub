Brick Buying Guide: Sizes & Mortar

Estimating brickwork is tricky because the mortar takes up space. If you simply divide the wall area by the brick size, you will buy way too many bricks. You must account for the 3/8" gap between every single brick.

Which Mortar Type? (N vs S)

When buying pre-mix bags at the store, you will see letters on the bag. These indicate the compressive strength.

Type N (Normal)

Best for general DIY. Use for exterior walls, chimneys, and veneers. It has medium strength and flexibility.

Type S (Structural)

High strength (1800 psi). Use for foundations, retaining walls, or paving/patios where the brick touches the ground.

Essential Accessories (Do Not Forget!)

A brick wall is more than just bricks and mortar. If you are building a wall against a house (Veneer), you need these safety components:

  • Corrugated Wall Ties: Small metal strips that nail into your house framing and sit in the mortar joint. They prevent the brick wall from peeling away from the house. Install one every 16 inches.
  • Weep Holes: Bricks are porous; water gets behind them. You must leave a small gap (weep hole) in the mortar at the bottom course every 24 inches so water can drain out.

Sand Selection: Masonry vs. Play Sand

⚠️ Never Use "Play Sand"

If you are mixing your own mortar, do not buy the cheap "Play Sand" meant for sandboxes. The grains are round and smooth, meaning the mortar will be weak and slide off your trowel.

Always buy "Masonry Sand" (also called Sharp Sand). The grains are jagged and lock together for a strong bond.

What is Efflorescence?

If you see a white, powdery substance appear on your new brick wall after it rains, don't panic. This is called Efflorescence .

It is simply salt migrating out of the brick/mortar as water evaporates. It usually washes away naturally after a few months, or you can clean it with a mild vinegar solution and a stiff brush.
