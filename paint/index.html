Paint Buying Guide: Texture & Sheen

Estimating paint isn't just about wall size. The texture of your wall and the color change determine how much paint you actually buy.

Why Texture Matters

Smooth Drywall

One gallon covers approx 350-400 sq. ft. This is the standard number on the can.

Textured / Popcorn

The bumps increase surface area. One gallon only covers approx 250-300 sq. ft. You need 20-30% more paint.

The "Fresh Drywall" Rule

New drywall acts like a sponge. If you paint directly onto it, the paper facing sucks the moisture out of the paint, leading to an uneven "flashing" finish.

  • Solution: Use a PVA Primer (Polyvinyl Acetate). It's cheap and seals the paper.
  • Quantity: Buy 1 gallon of primer for every gallon of wall paint you plan to use.

Sheen Guide: What to Buy?

Sheen Best For
Flat / Matte Ceilings (hides imperfections). Not washable.
Eggshell Living Rooms & Bedrooms. Lightly washable.
Satin Kitchens & Bathrooms. Moisture resistant and durable.
Semi-Gloss Trim, Doors, and Baseboards. Very tough & shiny.

Roller Selection (Don't Ignore This!)

Using the wrong roller thickness (nap) will ruin your finish.

  • 3/8 inch Nap: For standard smooth walls.
  • 1/2 inch Nap: For lightly textured walls.
  • 3/4 inch Nap: For stucco, brick, or popcorn ceilings.

💡 Pro Tip: "Box" Your Paint

If you buy 3 gallons of the same color, the shade might vary slightly between cans.

Always mix all 3 gallons together in a large 5-gallon bucket before you start. This guarantees the color is perfectly consistent across the whole room.
