Decking Guide: Wood vs. Composite

Building a deck is a big investment. The biggest choice you will make is the material. It affects the cost, the look, and how much maintenance you'll do every summer.

Material Comparison

Pressure Treated (PT)

  • Cost: $ (Cheapest)
  • Pros: Real wood look, structural strength.
  • Cons: Cracks, warps, splinters. Needs staining every 2 years.

Composite (Trex/TimberTech)

  • Cost: $$$ (3x more expensive)
  • Pros: Zero maintenance, no splinters, hidden fasteners.
  • Cons: Can get hot in the sun, synthetic feel.

Fasteners: Screws vs. Hidden Clips

Wood Decks: You typically use coated deck screws (2.5" or 3"). You drive them right through the face of the board (Face Screwing). Two screws per joist.

Composite Decks: Most pros use "Hidden Fasteners." These are plastic T-clips that slide into the groove on the side of the board. They screw into the joist, leaving the top surface smooth and hole-free.

Diagonal vs. Straight Layout

Laying deck boards at a 45-degree angle looks custom and high-end, but it comes with two costs:

  • More Waste: You have to cut the angle on every single board end. Expect 15-20% waste instead of the standard 5-10%.
  • Closer Joists: Standard decking spans 16" joists. But on a diagonal, the span is longer. You usually need to frame your joists at 12" on center to support a diagonal floor.

Pro Tip: Use Joist Tape

Before you lay your deck boards, stick Butyl Joist Tape on top of the wooden framing. This prevents water from sitting on the flat top of the joist and rotting the screws out. It adds 20 years to the life of the frame for about $100.
