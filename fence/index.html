Fence Building Guide: Privacy vs. Picket

A good fence makes good neighbors, but only if it doesn't fall down in a windstorm. The most critical part of your fence is the skeleton: the posts and rails.

Post Spacing: 6ft vs. 8ft

8 Foot Spacing

Standard for most residential fences. It saves money on posts and concrete. Good for 4ft picket fences or protected areas.

6 Foot Spacing (Recommended)

Required for 6ft+ tall Privacy fences in windy areas. The shorter span prevents the rails from sagging over time.

Rail Logic: 2 or 3 Rails?

The rails are the horizontal 2x4s that hold the pickets.

  • 4ft Fence: Needs 2 rails (Top and Bottom).
  • 6ft Fence: Needs 3 rails (Top, Middle, Bottom). The middle rail stops the pickets from warping.
  • 8ft Fence: Needs 4 rails.

The Frost Line Rule

Important: Your fence posts must be dug deeper than the "Frost Line" in your area.

If the ground freezes 24 inches down, your concrete footing must be 30 inches deep. If you don't do this, the freezing water will heave the post out of the ground in winter, ruining your fence.

Gate Hardware

Don't forget to budget for the gate. The gate post carries heavy loads and often needs to be a 6x6 post instead of a 4x4 for stability.

  • Hinges: Use heavy-duty T-Hinges.
  • Latch: Gravity latches are standard.
  • Anti-Sag Kit: A diagonal cable with a turnbuckle. Mandatory for wooden gates to keep them square.
