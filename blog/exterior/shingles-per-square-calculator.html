How Many Bundles Per Square? The 3-to-1 Rule

Field Notes

  • The Golden Rule: For standard architectural shingles, 3 Bundles = 1 Square (100 sq. ft.).
  • The "Waste" Factor: A simple gable roof needs 10% extra. A complex hip roof needs 15% extra for cuts.
  • Starter Strips: Don't use good shingles for the first row. Buy specialized "Starter Shingles" to seal the eave against wind uplift.

In the construction world, we speak two different languages.

When you go to the lumber yard, you buy shingles by the "Bundle" because that's what one guy can carry up a ladder on his shoulder.

But when a contractor quotes a job, he speaks in "Squares." This disconnect is the #1 reason homeowners get confused by their estimates or end up returning 20 heavy bags of asphalt to Home Depot.

A roof isn't just about covering plywood; it's about water management. If you run short on shingles, you can't just "stretch" them. You have to stop work, leaving your roof exposed to the rain. Today, I'm going to teach you the roofing math that keeps you dry.

Vocabulary Lesson: What is a "Square"?

If you call a roofing supply house and ask for "2,000 square feet of shingles," they will know you are a rookie. In this trade, we measure everything in Squares .

The Golden Formula

1 Square =
100 Sq. Ft.

Imagine a 10-foot by 10-foot section of your roof. That is one square. If your roof is 2,400 square feet total, you have a 24 Square roof.

The Bundle Ratio (3 vs. 4)

A "Bundle" is simply the package shingles come in. It is designed to weigh roughly 70-80 lbs so a human can lift it. Because different shingles have different thicknesses, the number of bundles per square changes.

1. The "Windbreaker" (Starter Strips)

If you nail your first row of shingles directly to the deck, water will leak through the seams. You need a base layer.

Old-school roofers used to just flip a 3-tab shingle upside down. Don't do this. Modern "Starter Strips" have a specialized adhesive tar line that seals your first row down so the wind doesn't peel your roof up like a banana.

MATH: Measure the perimeter of your eaves (bottom edges). Divide by 100 to get bundles of Starter.

2. The "Crown" (Hip & Ridge)

You can't bend a normal architectural shingle over the peak of the roof; it will crack. You need "Hip & Ridge" caps.

The Cheap Way

Buy bundles of standard 3-Tab Shingles (color matched). Cut them into three pieces. Use those as caps.

Cost: Low

The Pro Way

Buy specialized High-Profile Ridge Caps . They are thicker, pre-bent, and give the roof a defined, quality look.

Cost: High (But worth it)

3. The Hardware (Nails)

The "6-Nail" Rule

Standard code requires 4 nails per shingle. But if you live in a hurricane zone or high-wind area, use 6 nails per shingle . It voids the warranty if you don't.

Roofing FAQ: The Honest Truth

Can I just nail new shingles over the old ones?
Technically? Yes, code often allows two layers. **Practically? NO.**

A "layover" traps heat, which cooks the new shingles and cuts their lifespan in half. Worse, it prevents you from inspecting the plywood deck for rot. If you deck is rotten, new shingles won't fix the leak. Tear it off. Always.
How much "waste" should I buy?
It depends on your roof complexity.
  • Simple Gable (2 sides): add 10%
  • Hip Roof (4 sides): add 15%
  • Complex (Dormers/Valleys): add 20%
You will cut a lot of shingles to fit the valleys. That's waste.
Do I need felt paper (underlayment)?
**Absolutely.** Shingles shed water, but they aren't waterproof (wind can blow rain under them). The synthetic underlayment is your actual waterproof barrier. Don't skip it.

The Verdict

Roofing is a numbers game. Measure your footprint, multiply by your pitch, add your waste factor, and then divide by 3 (for bundles). If you get the math wrong, you'll be making a panic run to Home Depot while a storm rolls in. Measure twice, order once.
