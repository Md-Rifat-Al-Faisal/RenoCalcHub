Paver Sand vs. Gravel Base: Why You Need Both

The Hardscape Briefing

  • The Myth: "I'll just put sand on the dirt and lay the pavers."
    Result: Your patio will be wavy and sunken in 6 months.
  • The Gravel Job: Compacted gravel (crusher run) provides the structural strength to hold weight without sinking.
  • The Sand Job: A thin 1-inch layer of sand acts as a "mattress" to smooth out the roughness of the gravel so pavers sit flat.

I get asked this question once a week: "Sam, can I save money and just use sand under my pavers?"

My answer is always the same: Only if you like stepping in puddles.

Sand has zero structural integrity. If you walk on deep sand, your foot sinks. If you park a car on deep sand, it buries itself. If you lay pavers on deep sand, they will shift, tilt, and sink the first time it rains.

A proper patio isn't built on sand; it's built on rock . The sand is just the icing on the cake. Today, I’m going to break down the specific layers you need so your patio stays flat for 20 years, not 20 minutes.

The Base Crew Kit

You are essentially building a road. You need heavy equipment to do it right.

  • Plate Compactor (Rental) Do not use a hand tamper. You cannot compact 6 inches of gravel by hand. Rent this machine.

Layer 1: The Muscle (Gravel Base)

The gravel layer does 90% of the work. It supports the weight of your furniture, your BBQ grill, and your guests. It prevents the ground from heaving during freezes.

What to buy: Ask for "Crusher Run" (also called Road Base, QA, or 21A).

Why? It is a mix of 3/4-inch jagged stones AND stone dust. When you run a plate compactor over it, the dust fills the gaps between the stones, and it locks together into a solid, concrete-like slab. Regular "washed gravel" (just stones) will never lock; it stays loose forever.

Layer 2: The Mattress (Bedding Sand)

STOP! Do Not Buy "Play Sand"

The biggest rookie mistake is buying the soft, white sand used for children's sandboxes.

Play sand particles are round and smooth . They roll against each other like marbles. If you lay pavers on them, the bricks will wiggle.
The Solution: Buy "Concrete Sand" (or Coarse Sand). The particles are jagged and angular. They bite into each other and hold the brick firm.

The Golden Rule: 1 Inch MAX

This is where DIYers fail. They dump 3 or 4 inches of sand to make leveling easier. That is a death sentence for your patio.

1 Inch of Sand

The sand is firm. When you step on the paver corner, the energy is transferred straight down to the hard gravel base. The patio feels solid.

3+ Inches of Sand

The sand acts like a sponge. When you step on the corner, the sand shifts sideways. The paver tips. Water collects in the dip. The patio becomes wavy.

Rule #3: The PVC Rail Trick

You cannot level sand perfectly with a rake. It’s impossible. You need "rails."

The Trick: Buy two lengths of 1-inch PVC pipe (exterior diameter is usually exactly 1 inch). Lay them down on top of your compacted gravel base about 6 feet apart.

Dump your sand between the pipes. Take a straight 2x4 board, rest it on top of the pipes, and drag it backwards. The pipes act as a depth gauge. You will be left with a flawless, glass-smooth layer of sand exactly 1 inch thick.

The Process

  1. 1. Lay pipes on compacted gravel.
  2. 2. Overfill area with Concrete Sand.
  3. 3. Drag 2x4 board along pipes to scrape off excess.
  4. 4. Lift pipes and fill the grooves with a handful of sand.

Rule #4: The Floor is Lava

DO NOT WALK ON THE SAND

Once you screed the sand smooth, do not touch it.

If you step on it, you create a divot. That divot will cause the paver above it to sit low. You must lay the pavers while standing on the gravel (or on the pavers you already laid), leaning forward to place the next one gently onto the sand.

Rule #5: Compact LATER

Common Mistake: People try to compact the sand layer before laying bricks.
The Fix: Do NOT compact the bedding sand alone. Lay the pavers first. Then, run the plate compactor over the pavers (with a protective pad). This vibrates the sand up into the joints from below, locking everything tight.

Paver Base FAQ

Can I use Stone Dust instead of Sand?
Ideally, no. Stone dust traps water. In cold climates, that water freezes and heaves the pavers. Concrete sand drains water instantly. Stone dust is okay for natural flagstone, but for standard concrete pavers, stick to sand.
Is this "Polymeric Sand"?
NO. Polymeric sand is expensive glue-sand used between the joints on top to stop weeds. Bedding sand (Concrete Sand) goes underneath . Do not waste money putting polymeric sand underneath!
Do I need Landscape Fabric?
Yes, but location matters. Put the fabric on the bare dirt before you pour the gravel. This prevents the gravel from sinking into the mud. Do NOT put fabric between the gravel and the sand—it creates a slip plane.

The Verdict

There is no "versus." You need both. 6 inches of compacted angular gravel for strength, and 1 inch of concrete sand for smoothing. Skip either one, and you aren't building a patio—you're building a future repair job.
