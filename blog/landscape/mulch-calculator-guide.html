How Much Mulch Do I Need? Bulk vs. Bags

The Soil Briefing

  • The Magic Number: 1 Cubic Yard = 13.5 bags (2 cu ft size). If you need more than 20 bags, buy bulk.
  • The Depth Rule: 3 inches is perfect. Less allows weeds; more suffocates the roots.
  • Don't Volcano: Never pile mulch against a tree trunk. It causes rot. Leave a "donut" of air around the base.

Every spring, I see the same thing at the hardware store. A homeowner is loading 40 bags of mulch into a sedan, exhausting themselves and spending twice as much money as they need to.

Mulch is volume business. When you buy by the bag, you are paying for the plastic, the branding, and the pallet shipping. When you buy in bulk (by the cubic yard), you are paying for the wood.

But ordering bulk is intimidating. What if you order a dump truck full and it's way too much? What if it blocks your driveway for weeks? Today, we are going to do the math so you order exactly the right amount to blanket your soil without burying your driveway.

The Spreading Kit

Spreading mulch is aerobic exercise. The right tools save your lower back.

  • Bedding Fork (Pitchfork) Do not use a shovel. A fork pierces the pile and moves twice the material with half the effort.
  • 4-Wheel Dump Cart Better than a wheelbarrow because it doesn't tip over. Crucial for long distances.

Rule #1: The Bag vs. Bulk Math

Here is the only number you need to memorize: 13.5 .

That is how many standard bags (2 cubic feet) fit into one "scoop" (1 cubic yard) of bulk mulch.

If your garden needs 20 bags, you are essentially buying a yard and a half. At that point, you are paying a premium for plastic packaging that you're just going to throw away.

Rule #3: The "Goldilocks" Depth (3 Inches)

Mulch is a blanket. Too thin, and it doesn't work. Too thick, and it suffocates the plants.

1 Inch (Too Thin)

Sunlight penetrates through to the soil. Weeds germinate immediately. It washes away in the first rain. Waste of money.

3 Inches (Perfect)

Blocks sunlight (no weeds). Retains moisture. Allows oxygen to reach the roots. This is the biological sweet spot.

5+ Inches (Deadly)

Suffocates the soil. Water can't penetrate light rains. Fungal mats form. Roots grow upward seeking air and dry out.

Rule #4: Stop the Volcanoes

I see this everywhere: landscaping crews piling mulch up against the trunk of a tree like a volcano.

This kills the tree. Tree bark is meant to be dry. If you cover it with wet mulch, it rots. Insects move in. The tree slowly dies.

The Fix: Leave a 3-inch gap (a donut hole) around the trunk where you can see bare flare.

Mulch is Food, Not Paint

When you buy mulch, you are making a choice: Are you feeding your soil, or are you just painting the ground?

Organic mulch (bark/wood) breaks down over time. This is a good thing. As it decomposes, it turns into compost, feeding earthworms and improving your clay soil. Inorganic mulch (rubber/stone) sits there forever, doing nothing for the soil biology.

Double Shredded Hardwood (The Gold Standard)

What is it? Tree bark that has been shredded twice. It is fibrous and fuzzy.
Pros: It "knits" together, so it won't wash away on slopes. Break down creates rich soil.
Cons: Fades to gray in about 6-9 months.

Sam's Choice

Dyed Mulch (Black/Red/Brown)

What is it? Often recycled wood (pallets/lumber) sprayed with iron oxide (red) or carbon (black) dye.
Pros: Holds color for 12+ months. Looks sharp and modern.
Cons: It doesn't feed the soil as well as bark. It takes longer to break down (nitrogen starvation).

Cedar Mulch (The Bug Repellent)

What is it? Shredded cedar wood (smells great).
Pros: Natural oils repel termites, ticks, and fleas. Rot-resistant (lasts longer).
Cons: More expensive. The light color can look "messy" faster than dark mulch.

The "Do Not Use" List

Rubber Mulch

Why? It's shredded tires. It gets dangerously hot in the summer (cooking plant roots). It smells like a race track. It never decomposes, so you are stuck with it forever. Only use this for playgrounds, never gardens.

Fresh Wood Chips

Why? Wood chips straight from a tree service haven't aged. As they start to decompose, they suck all the Nitrogen out of the soil to fuel the process, starving your flowers (Nitrogen Tie-up). Always use aged mulch.

Pro Warning: Artillery Fungus

If you have white vinyl siding or a white car, listen up.

Cheap, wood-based mulch (not bark) can breed Artillery Fungus ( Sphaerobolus ). This fungus shoots tiny, sticky black spores up to 20 feet.

These black dots stick to your siding and car like superglue. They do not scrub off. The only way to remove them is often to scrape the paint.

Prevention: Use Hardwood Bark Mulch (not wood chips) or Cedar. The fungus hates bark.

Mulch FAQ: The Maintenance

Does mulch attract termites?
Termites are already in the ground; mulch doesn't "summon" them. However, mulch provides the moisture they love.
The Rule: Keep mulch **6 inches away** from your home's foundation or siding. Leave a strip of bare dirt or gravel near the house so termites can't bridge the gap into your walls unseen.
Should I remove old mulch before adding new?
Usually, no. Organic mulch decomposes and feeds the soil. If the old layer has rotted down to less than an inch, just mulch over it.
Exception: If you have 4+ inches of old, crusty mulch that hasn't broken down, rake some out before adding more. Do not exceed the 3-inch total depth.
What is "Sour Mulch"?
If a massive pile of mulch sits too long without air (anaerobic), it creates acetic acid. It smells like **vinegar or rotten eggs**.
The Fix: Do not put this on your plants; it will burn them. Spread it out on a tarp in the sun for 24 hours to let the gases escape and the rain rinse it. Once the smell is gone, it's safe.

The Verdict

Stop buying 50 bags of plastic waste every spring. Measure your beds, apply the "13.5 Bags per Yard" rule, and order bulk hardwood mulch. It feeds your soil, looks better, and saves your back. Just remember: 3 inches deep, keep it off the tree trunks, and never use rubber.
