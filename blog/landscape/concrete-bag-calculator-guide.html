How Many 80lb Bags for a 10x10 Slab? The "Back-Breaking" Math

The Short Answer

  • The Magic Number: For a 10' x 10' slab at 4 inches thick, the math says 45 bags (80lb).
  • The "Real World" Number: Buy 50 bags . Uneven ground and spillage always eat up material.
  • The Weight Warning: That is 4,000 lbs. Do not try to mix this in a wheelbarrow unless you have a football team helping you.

I have seen grown men cry over a 10x10 slab.

It starts innocently. You think, "It's just a small patio." You buy 30 bags because that looked like a lot at the store. You start mixing. Halfway through, you realize you aren't even close to filling the frame.

Now you have a problem. Wet concrete starts hardening in about 90 minutes. If you run to the store for more, the first half will cure before you get back. When you pour the fresh wet mix against the hardening dry mix, you create a "Cold Joint." That is a permanent crack waiting to happen.

Concrete waits for no man. Today, we are going to do the volume math so you can buy the right amount, rent the right mixer, and finish the job before your arms fall off.

The Mason's Kit

For a project this size, a shovel isn't enough. You need mechanical help.

  • Portable Cement Mixer For 45+ bags, RENT or buy this. Mixing by hand in a tub will destroy your back.
  • Magnesium Float Crucial for smoothing the wet concrete and bringing the "cream" to the top.

Rule #1: The "Inches-to-Feet" Trap

Here is where 90% of DIYers fail. They multiply $10 \times 10 \times 4$ and get 400. They think they need 400 something.

The Problem: Your length is in feet , but your depth is in inches . You cannot multiply apples by oranges. You must convert the depth into feet first.

// THE CORRECT FORMULA

4 inches ÷ 12 = 0.33 feet

10' (L) × 10' (W) × 0.33' (D) = 33.3 Cubic Feet

You need to fill 33.3 cubic feet of space. Now, let's see how much space one bag fills.

Rule #2: Bag Yield (80lb vs 60lb)

A bag of concrete is not 1 cubic foot. It is much less. The "Yield" is the volume of the wet concrete after you mix it.

Bag Size Yield (Cu. Ft.) Bags Needed (33.3 cu ft)
80 lb (Standard) 0.60 56 Bags
60 lb (Light) 0.45 74 Bags
50 lb (Fast) 0.37 90 Bags

Rule #3: The "Grade Tax"

The Ground isn't Flat

You dug the hole, but it isn't laser-perfect.

If your depth varies from 3.5" to 4.5", you will use more concrete than the math predicts. Concrete fills every void. We call this the "Grade Tax." Always add 10% to your total.

Math: 56 Bags + 10% (5.6) = 62 Bags

The 4,000 lb Problem

Let’s be real. 62 bags of 80lb concrete is 4,960 pounds . That is the weight of a Ford F-150 truck.

If you try to mix that in a wheelbarrow with a hoe, you will fail. Best case scenario: You finish the slab, but it looks terrible because the first batch dried before you poured the last batch. Worst case: You blow a disc in your back.

Rule #3: The "Rule of 10"

Under 10 Bags

Method: Wheelbarrow & Hoe.
Crew: 1 Person.
Vibe: "A good workout."

10 to 60 Bags

Method: Rent a Portable Mixer.
Crew: 2-3 People.
Vibe: "Hard work, but doable."

Over 60 Bags (10x12 Slab+)

Method: Call a Ready-Mix Truck.
Crew: 3+ People.
Reason: At 60 bags, the cost of the bags equals the "short load fee" of a truck. The truck pours it in 15 minutes. You save your back for free.

The 3-Person Crew

You cannot do a 10x10 slab alone. The concrete cures too fast. You need an assembly line.

  • 1
    The Mixer (The Beast) Stays at the pile. Cuts bags, feeds the mixer, adds water. Never leaves the machine.
  • 2
    The Mule (The Runner) Dumps the mixed concrete into a wheelbarrow, runs it to the slab, dumps it, runs back. Cardio day.
  • 3
    The Finisher (The Artist) Spreads the wet mud (screeding), taps out air bubbles, and floats the surface smooth.

The Water Trap

CRITICAL: Do not make "Concrete Soup."

Beginners add too much water because it makes the mix easier to shovel.
The Fact: Too much water weakens the concrete. It causes cracking and "dusting" (where the top layer flakes off forever). The mix should look like thick oatmeal , not soup. It should hold its shape when you pile it up.

Concrete FAQ: The Details

Do I need Rebar or Wire Mesh?
For a simple patio, heavy #4 Rebar is usually overkill. However, standard **Wire Mesh** (Remesh) is excellent for preventing cracks from spreading.

Sam's Pro Tip: Use "Fiber Reinforced" concrete bags. They have millions of tiny fiberglass hairs mixed in. It saves you from cutting and placing rusty wire mesh.
What if it rains while pouring?
Light mist is fine. Heavy rain will ruin the surface finish (it creates craters). Always have a roll of **6-mil plastic sheeting** nearby. If the sky opens up, cover the slab gently and wait it out.
How long before I can walk on it?
24 to 48 hours for foot traffic.
7 days for patio furniture or a hot tub.
28 days for full cure (if you plan to stain or paint it).
Crucial: Keep the slab wet with a garden hose mist for the first 3 days to make it harder ("Water Curing").

The Verdict

Concrete is cheap, but the labor is expensive. Don't risk ruining $300 of material by running short. For a 10x10 slab, buy 62 bags, rent the mixer, and invite three strong friends. If you have 5 bags left over? Return them. If you are 5 bags short? You have a permanent scar on your patio.
