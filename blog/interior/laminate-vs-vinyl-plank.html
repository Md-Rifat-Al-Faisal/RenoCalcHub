The Essential Math of Flooring

The biggest mistake I see in my 18 years is homeowners ordering the exact square footage of the room. Wood-look planks are rectangles being fitted into rooms that are almost never perfectly square. Between "cutting in" around door frames and the pieces you lose at the end of a row, your "net" area is never your "gross" order.

For standard parallel installation, you must add a 10% waste factor . If you are doing a herringbone pattern or a 45-degree diagonal layout (which I love for making small rooms feel larger), you need to bump that to 15-20% .

5 Costly Mistakes Beginners Make

Flatness Check

.

Over my 18 years in high-end remodeling, I’ve seen homeowners drop $5,000 on materials only to have the floor fail in six months. It’s almost never the material's fault—it’s the execution. Avoid these traps:

1. Skipping the "Flatness" Check

Vinyl and laminate are "floating floors." If your subfloor has a dip or a hump of more than **3/16 inch over a 10-foot span**, the locking joints will eventually snap under the pressure of foot traffic. If you hear a "clicking" or "popping" sound when you walk, your floor wasn't flat enough.

2. Tight expansion gaps

Both materials expand and contract. If you install planks tight against the wall without the mandatory **1/4 to 3/8 inch gap**, the floor will hit the wall as it expands in summer and "peak" (buckle) in the middle of the room.

Pro Tip: Always use spacers around the perimeter. Baseboards will cover the gap later.

3. Ignoring the "Toilet Leak" Reality

Homeowners often choose laminate for bathrooms because it "looks like wood." Even "Waterproof" laminate has a wood-composite core. If a toilet overflows, water sits under the planks. Only **Luxury Vinyl (LVP)** is truly safe for full-bath environments.

4. Cheap Underlayment

The "hollow" sound people hate about laminate usually comes from cheap, thin underlayment. Investing an extra $0.50 per square foot in **high-density foam or cork** makes a $2 floor feel like a $10 floor.

The "Final Board" Disaster

Most beginners forget that the last row of boards usually needs to be ripped lengthwise. If you didn't account for a **10-15% waste factor**, you will find yourself one board short on a Sunday evening. Don't let a $50 box of flooring stand between you and a finished home.

Elena's Professional Secret

"Before you install a single plank, acclimate the boxes. Keep the unopened boxes in the room for **48 to 72 hours**. If you take cold laminate from a warehouse and install it in a warm house immediately, the joints will pull apart within a week as the material shrinks."

Project FAQ

Does laminate or vinyl have better resale value?
Historically, **laminate** was seen as a "cheap" option, but high-end 12mm laminate now rivals hardwood in looks. However, in 2025, **Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)** is the market favorite because buyers love the "waterproof" peace of mind. For a rental or a family home, LVP usually wins on resale.
Can I install new planks over my existing tile?
**Yes**, provided the tile is flat and the grout lines aren't too deep. If the grout lines are wider than 1/4 inch, you must fill them with a leveler first, or the new planks will eventually "telegraph" the pattern of the tiles underneath.
How do I clean these floors without damaging them?
**Never use a steam mop** on laminate; it forced moisture into the joints. For both floors, a microfiber mop with a pH-neutral cleaner is best. Avoid wax or "shine-enhancing" products as they build up a cloudy film over time.
Which floor is better for large dogs?
High-quality **Laminate** actually has a higher "AC Rating" (scratch resistance) than vinyl. If your concern is scratches from claws, go with an AC4 or AC5 rated laminate. If your concern is "accidents" or water bowls, vinyl is the safer bet.
