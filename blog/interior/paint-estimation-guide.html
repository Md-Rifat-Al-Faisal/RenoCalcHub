How to Estimate Paint: Stop Buying Extra Gallons

The Designer's Briefing

  • The Golden Rule: One gallon covers 350 to 400 square feet (one coat). Always calculate for TWO coats.
  • The "Void" Math: Don't pay to paint windows. Subtract 15 sq. ft. for every window and 20 sq. ft. for every door.
  • The Batch Warning: If you run out halfway through a wall, the new gallon might be a slightly different shade. Buy all your paint at once.

Picture this: It’s 4:00 PM on a Saturday. You are exhausted. You are rolling the final coat on the last wall of your living room. Suddenly, your roller tray runs dry. You squeeze the last drop out of the can, but it’s not enough. You have three feet of wall left to paint.

Now you have to wash your brushes, drive to the store, and spend $50 on a new gallon just to use one cup of it.

On the flip side, I see homeowners buying five gallons for a project that only needed three. That is $100 of wasted money sitting in your garage. Estimating paint isn't magic—it is a simple formula of surface area minus voids.

The Measuring Kit

Put down the phone estimation app (they are notoriously inaccurate). Grab these physical tools.

The Calculation Formula

You need to think in rectangles. Every wall is just a big rectangle (Length x Height), and every window is a small rectangle you subtract.

The Paint Formula Visualized

(Length × Height) minus (Windows + Doors) = Paintable Area
1
Measure the Perimeter

Add up the length of all 4 walls.
Example: 12' + 10' + 12' + 10' = 44 linear feet.

2
Multiply by Height

Usually 8 feet.
Example: 44' x 8' = 352 total square feet.

3
Subtract the "Holes"

Don't do complex math. Just subtract 20 sq ft per door and 15 sq ft per window .

Don't Forget Sheen & Primer

Estimating isn't just about gallons; it's about buying the right gallons. If you buy 5 gallons of Flat paint for a house with kids, you might as well throw money in the trash because you can't clean it.

Glossier paints cover slightly more area but show more wall imperfections.

Buy Primer If...

  • • You are painting over new, raw drywall .
  • • You are painting Light over Dark (White over Navy).
  • • You are covering stains (crayon, smoke, water).

Skip Primer If...

  • • The walls are already painted and in good condition.
  • • The color change is minor (e.g., Light Grey to Dark Grey).
  • • You are using premium "Paint-and-Primer-in-One".

Estimation FAQ

Does one gallon cover a standard 12x12 room?
It cuts it very close. A 12x12 room with 8-foot ceilings has roughly 384 square feet of wall space. Subtract a door and window, and you are at ~350 square feet. Since a gallon covers ~350-400 sq ft, one gallon will only give you one coat . To do the job right (two coats), you will need two gallons.
What is the "Waste Factor"?
Paint gets stuck in the roller nap (up to a cup!), spills on the drop cloth, or dries in the tray. Always add 10% to your final calculation to account for waste.
Can I return unopened paint?
Usually, no . Once paint is tinted to your specific color, the store cannot resell it. This is why accurate estimation is crucial. However, un-tinted primers or factory-white ceiling paints are often returnable if unopened.

The Verdict

There is nothing more frustrating than pausing a renovation project because you ran out of paint. By taking five minutes to measure your room and accounting for two coats plus waste, you save yourself time, money, and stress. Ready to get the exact number?
