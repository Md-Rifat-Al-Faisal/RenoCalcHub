Buying Advice:

Why AC Sizing Matters (The Goldilocks Rule)

[Image of AC unit parts]

Most people assume "Bigger is Better." This is wrong. An AC unit acts as a dehumidifier. If you buy a massive unit for a small room, it cools the air too fast and shuts off before it can remove the moisture.

  • Too Big: Short cycling (on/off constantly). Leaves room cold but "clammy" and humid. High electric bills.
  • Too Small: Runs 24/7 without ever reaching target temp. Compressor burns out early.
  • Just Right: Runs for 15-20 minute cycles, removing heat and humidity efficiently.

Unit Types Guide

Window Unit

Best for single rooms under 500 sq ft. Cheap & Easy.

Portable AC

Least efficient. Only use if window units are banned by HOA/Landlord.

Mini Split

High efficiency, quiet, best for open floor plans. Requires pro install.

⚡ Voltage Check

Under 15,000 BTU: Usually fits standard 115 Volt outlets.
Over 15,000 BTU: Usually requires a special 230 Volt outlet (like a dryer plug). Check your wiring before buying!

Understanding SEER & CEER Ratings

The yellow "Energy Guide" sticker tells you how much the unit will cost to run.

Standard Efficiency

Most cheap window units have a CEER of 10.0 to 10.9 . These are fine for occasional use.

High Efficiency (Energy Star)

Look for a CEER of 12.0 or higher . They cost $50 more upfront but save about $20-$40 per year in electricity.

Inverter vs. Standard Compressors

This is the biggest innovation in AC technology in 20 years.

  • Standard AC: Like a light switch. It is either 100% ON (noisy) or 100% OFF. The temperature fluctuates.
  • Inverter AC: Like a dimmer switch. It can run at 20% power just to maintain the temperature. It is whisper quiet and saves 30% energy. Highly recommended for bedrooms.

Maintenance: The "Filter Rule"

Clean your filter every 2 weeks during summer. A clogged filter reduces airflow, forcing the AC to work 2x harder. This increases your bill and can cause the internal coils to freeze into a block of ice.
