Laminate & Vinyl Plank Buying Guide

Buying flooring is tricky because it is sold in boxes, not by the foot. To avoid running out halfway through the job (and facing mismatched dye lots), you must calculate the correct "Waste Factor."

Which Waste Factor Should I Use?

5%

Rectangular Rooms (Pro)

If your room is a perfect square and you are very experienced with a saw, you can get away with 5% waste.

10%

Standard DIY (Recommended)

The industry standard. This covers mistakes, bad cuts, and the inevitable "end of row" waste.

15%

Complex Layouts

If you have angled walls, multiple closets, or are laying the floor diagonally, you need 15% extra material.

What is the "AC Rating"?

For laminate flooring, the Abrasion Class (AC) tells you how durable it is. Don't buy a low rating for a high-traffic area.

Rating Best For
AC3 All Residential Use (Living rooms, Bedrooms). Standard choice.
AC4 Light Commercial (Offices, Cafes). Very durable for homes.
AC5 Heavy Commercial (Department stores). Extremely tough.

The 48-Hour Acclimatization Rule

NEVER install flooring the day you buy it.

Wood and laminate expand and contract with temperature. You must leave the unopened boxes in the room where they will be installed for at least 48 hours . If you skip this, your floor will buckle or gap within a month.

Don't Forget: Underlayment & Molding

  • Underlayment: If your planks don't have "attached pad," you must buy rolls of foam underlayment. Buy the same square footage as your room area.
  • Quarter Round / Shoe Molding: You need to cover the expansion gap at the walls. Measure the Perimeter of your room to know how many linear feet to buy.
  • Transitions: Count your doorways. You will need a "T-Molding" or "Reducer" for every door transition.

💡 Pro Tip: Shuffle the Boxes

When installing, open 3 boxes at once and mix the planks. This blends any slight color variations between boxes, making the floor look natural instead of "patchy."
