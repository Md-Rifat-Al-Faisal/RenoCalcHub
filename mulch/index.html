Mulch & Soil Buying Guide

A fresh layer of mulch makes any garden look professional, but it also serves a purpose. It retains water, suppresses weeds, and insulates plant roots from freezing temperatures.

Which Material Should I Choose?

Shredded Hardwood Mulch

The most common choice. It mats down well (won't wash away in rain) and breaks down slowly to feed the soil.

Topsoil / Compost

Use this for filling holes, leveling low spots in your lawn, or mixing into existing dirt to plant flowers. Do NOT use it as a top layer for weed control.

Pea Gravel / Rock

Permanent solution. Great for walkways or drainage areas. Warning: It is extremely heavy. Do not load too many bags in a sedan.

Vehicle Capacity Guide

Before you head to the store, know your limit. Soil and gravel are much heavier than mulch.

Sedan ~800 lbs 15-20 Bags
Small SUV ~1,000 lbs 25 Bags
Pickup Truck (1/2 Ton) ~1,500 - 2,000 lbs 40-50 Bags (1 Pallet)

Avoid "Volcano Mulching"

The #1 Mistake: Piling mulch high up against the tree trunk like a volcano.

This traps moisture against the bark, causing the trunk to rot and encouraging insects/rodents to eat the tree. Correct Way: Leave a 2-3 inch gap between the mulch and the tree trunk. It should look like a donut, not a volcano.

Do I Need Landscape Fabric?

With Wood Mulch? NO.

Mulch is supposed to rot and feed the soil. Fabric blocks the nutrients from reaching the roots. Weeds will eventually grow on top of the fabric anyway.

With Rock/Gravel? YES.

Rocks sink into the mud over time. Fabric acts as a barrier to keep your expensive stones from disappearing into the dirt.

Is Dyed Mulch Safe?

The red and black dyes used in modern mulch are typically made from Iron Oxide (Rust) and Carbon (Charcoal) . These are safe for pets and plants.

However, ensure the wood itself is not recycled construction waste (like old decks), which might contain pressure-treated chemicals (CCA). Always buy bags labeled "MSC Certified" if you are unsure.
