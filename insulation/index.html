Insulation Guide: R-Value & Types

90% of US homes are under-insulated. Adding insulation to your attic is the highest ROI project you can do, often paying for itself in lower energy bills within 2-3 years.

What is R-Value?

The "R" stands for Resistance to heat flow. The higher the number, the better it keeps heat IN during winter and OUT during summer.

Zone Recommended Depth (Cellulose)
South (Hot) R-30 to R-38 8 - 10 Inches
Mid-Atlantic R-38 to R-49 10 - 14 Inches
North (Cold) R-49 to R-60 14 - 17 Inches

Blown-In vs. Rolls (Batts)

Fiberglass Rolls

Best For: New construction or open walls without drywall.
Cons: Hard to fit around wires and pipes. Gaps reduce efficiency drastically.

Blown-In Cellulose

Best For: Adding to existing attics ("Topping up").
Pros: It flows like liquid around obstacles, sealing every gap. You can rent the blower machine for free at most big-box stores if you buy 10+ bags.

Safety Warning

Fiberglass Itch: Always wear a N95 Respirator , goggles, and long sleeves. Fiberglass particles can damage your lungs and irritate your skin.

Don't Block Soffits: When adding insulation, make sure you don't cover the vents at the edge of the roof (Soffit Vents). Use "Baffles" (pink foam chutes) to keep the airflow open, or your roof will rot from trapped moisture.
