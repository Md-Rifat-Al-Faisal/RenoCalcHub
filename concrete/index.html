Concrete Buying Guide: Bags vs. Truck

Concrete math is unforgiving. If you order too little, you have a "cold joint" (a crack where the old wet concrete meets the new wet concrete). If you buy too much, you are stuck hauling heavy bags back to the store.

Which Bag Size Should I Buy?

80lb Bags (Yellow Bag)

Pros: Cheaper per pound. Less bags to open.
Cons: Very heavy. Difficult to lift into the mixer if you are working alone.

60lb Bags (Red/Orange Bag)

Pros: Manageable weight. Good for small patches.
Cons: More expensive per pound. You need to open more bags.

When to Order a Truck

The general rule is 1 Cubic Yard .

  • Under 1 Yard (approx 45 bags): Use bags and a portable electric mixer.
  • Over 1 Yard: Call a Ready-Mix company. The "Short Load" fee is worth it to avoid mixing 50+ bags by hand, which is physically exhausting and time-sensitive.

💧 Water Ratio Warning

The biggest mistake DIYers make is adding too much water. Soup-like concrete is weak and will crack. The mix should be the consistency of thick oatmeal —it should hold its shape when you pile it up.

Curing: The Secret to Strength

Concrete does not "dry"; it "cures" through a chemical reaction called hydration.

  • Keep it Wet: For 3 to 7 days after pouring, mist the slab with a garden hose or cover it with plastic sheeting.
  • Why? If the water evaporates too fast (on a hot day), the chemical reaction stops, and the concrete becomes brittle and dusty on top.

Reinforcement: Rebar vs. Mesh

Do you need metal inside your concrete?

Project Recommendation
Fence Post None needed.
Patio / Sidewalk Wire Mesh helps hold cracks together.
Driveway Rebar (#3 or #4) is mandatory to support vehicle weight.

Temperature Warning

Too Cold? Never pour if it will drop below 32°F (0°C) in the next 48 hours. The water inside will freeze and shatter the concrete.

Too Hot? If it's over 90°F (32°C) , the concrete will set too fast for you to finish. Use cold water and work quickly.
