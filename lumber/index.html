Board Feet vs. Linear Feet: A Woodworker's Guide

If you go to Home Depot, you buy wood by the "Linear Foot" (just the length). But if you go to a proper hardwood lumberyard to buy Walnut, Cherry, or Maple, they sell it by the "Board Foot" (BF). This confuses almost every beginner woodworker. This guide explains the math so you don't get ripped off at the lumberyard.

1 The Magic Formula

A Board Foot is a measurement of volume , not just length. Imagine a chunk of wood that is 12 inches wide, 12 inches long, and 1 inch thick. That is 1 BF.

(Thickness" × Width" × Length') ÷ 12

*Note: Length is in FEET, while Width/Thickness are in INCHES.

Why does this matter? A board that is 8 inches wide costs twice as much as a board that is 4 inches wide, even if they are the same length. The Board Foot system accounts for this width difference fairly.

2 Understanding "Quarters" (4/4, 8/4)

Lumberyards don't say "1 inch thick." They speak in fractions of a quarter-inch.

Lingo Rough Thickness Surfaced (Final)
4/4 1 Inch ~ 3/4 Inch
5/4 1.25 Inch ~ 1 Inch
8/4 2 Inches ~ 1.75 Inches

Important: You pay for the "Rough" thickness (e.g. 1 inch), even though the board you take home might be planed down to 3/4 inch. That wood turned into sawdust, but you still bought it!

3 S4S vs. Rough Sawn

Rough Sawn

Straight from the mill. Fuzzy surface, wavy edges.

CHEAPER

Requires: Jointer & Planer.

S4S (Surfaced 4 Sides)

Cleaned up, flat, and straight edges.

EXPENSIVE

Ready to use. Great for hobbyists.

The 20% Rule

Hardwood has defects. Knots, sapwood (white streaks in walnut), and cracks at the ends of boards (checking).

When estimating a project, always add 20-25% extra to your Board Foot calculation. If your table top is 20 BF, buy 25 BF of lumber. This allows you to cut around the ugly parts and match the grain perfectly.
